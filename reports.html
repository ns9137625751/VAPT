A report with two audiences: an executive summary a board can read, and a technical body an engineer can work straight from. Every finding reproduced, rated against CVSS v3.1, and paired with a specific fix.

What is in the document

One report, two audiences. The first ten pages are written for people who will never open a terminal; everything after that is written for the engineer who has to fix it.

[+] Executive summary Two pages. What we tested, what we found, what it means commercially, and what to do first. No jargon, no CVSS vectors.
[+] Risk overview Findings by severity, by asset and by category, with the trend against your previous engagement if there was one.
[+] Scope and method Exactly what was in scope, what was excluded, which standards were applied and where coverage was limited.
[+] Technical findings One entry per finding: summary, affected asset, CVSS vector, reproduction steps, evidence, impact and a specific fix.
[+] Remediation plan Findings ordered by severity and effort, so you can decide what ships this sprint and what waits.
[+] Observations Things that are not vulnerabilities but are worth knowing: hardening gaps, deprecated components, architectural risk.
[+] Appendices Full endpoint and host inventory, tooling and versions used, and the raw evidence index.
[+] Attestation letter A one-page signed summary you can send to customers and auditors without disclosing the technical detail.

A real finding, redacted

[!] CRITICAL VAPT-2026-0142
--------------------------------------------------------------
Title Insecure direct object reference on invoice retrieval
Asset api.example.com GET /api/v2/invoices/:id
CVSS 3.1 9.1 AV:N/AC:L/PR:L/UI:N/S:U/C:H/I:N/A:H
Status [x] reproduced evidence attached
SUMMARY
An authenticated user of any tenant can retrieve invoices belonging
to any other tenant by incrementing the numeric identifier. No
authorisation check ties :id to the caller's organisation.
REPRODUCTION
1. Authenticate as user-a@tenant-one (role: viewer)
2. GET /api/v2/invoices/10231 -> 200, own invoice
3. GET /api/v2/invoices/10232 -> 200, tenant-two invoice
4. Enumerated 1,000 ids in 41s; 994 returned foreign records
IMPACT
Full read access to billing records across all tenants, including
customer names, addresses and payment amounts. Reportable under
GDPR Art. 33 if exploited.
REMEDIATION
Scope the query to the caller's organisation rather than filtering
after retrieval:
- invoice = Invoice.find(params[:id])
+ invoice = current_user.organisation.invoices.find(params[:id])
Apply the same pattern to the 6 sibling endpoints listed in App. B.
RETEST [x] closed 2026-03-14 verified against all 7 endpoints

How we rate severity

Every finding carries a CVSS v3.1 base vector so you can compare it against anything else in your tracker. We also give our own rating, because CVSS does not know what your application is for.

[!] Critical 9.0+ Direct path to data loss or account takeover, exploitable now, no meaningful precondition. Reported the day we confirm it. fix in 24h
[!] High 7.0+ Serious impact, but needs a precondition, a chain, or a privileged position to reach. fix in 7d
[~] Medium 4.0+ Weakens your posture and shows up in audits. Frequently a configuration change rather than a code change. fix in 30d
[-] Low 0.1+ Hardening and hygiene. Worth batching into your next maintenance window rather than interrupting a sprint. next cycle
[ ] Info 0.0 Not a vulnerability. Recorded in observations because it may matter later or to your auditor. no action
