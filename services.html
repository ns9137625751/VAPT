What we test, and how deeply.

Six service lines, one methodology. Every engagement is fixed-scope, manually tested, and ends with a report your engineers can act on plus a free retest once they have.

[+] 6 service lines   [+] 4–15 day engagements   [+] Free retest within 30 days

[01] Web application testing

SPA, server-rendered, legacy · 5–10 days

Every authenticated role is tested by hand against every other role. Scanners establish the baseline; the findings that matter come from someone reasoning about your business logic.

[+] Injection SQL, NoSQL, command, template and LDAP injection across every sink the application reaches, including second-order paths.
[+] Cross-site scripting Stored, reflected and DOM-based XSS, plus the CSP and sanitiser gaps that let them through.
[+] Access control Horizontal and vertical privilege escalation, IDOR on every object reference, forced browsing and function-level gaps.
[+] Authentication Credential handling, session fixation, MFA bypass, password reset flows and account-recovery abuse.
[+] Business logic Race conditions, workflow skipping, price and quantity tampering, and abuse of your own rules against you.
[+] Client-side CSRF, clickjacking, postMessage handling, prototype pollution and dependency risk in the bundle you actually ship.
[x]

You receive: Report with reproduction steps per finding, a remediation per issue, and a retest within 30 days.

[02] API security testing

REST, GraphQL, gRPC · 4–8 days

We test every documented path and then go looking for the ones that are not documented. Your spec is a starting point, not the boundary of the assessment.

[+] Object-level authz Every identifier in every response probed against every other account. This is where most real breaches start.
[+] Token security JWT algorithm confusion, weak signing keys, missing audience and expiry checks, refresh-token replay.
[+] OAuth and OIDC Redirect-URI validation, PKCE, scope escalation and consent handling across your flows.
[+] Mass assignment Fields the client should never be able to set, found by diffing your models against what the endpoint accepts.
[+] Rate and quota Enumeration, credential stuffing and cost-amplification resistance, including GraphQL query depth and aliasing.
[+] Spec drift Endpoints that exist but are not in the schema, deprecated versions still answering, and debug routes left live.
[x]

You receive: Findings exported as JSON or SARIF alongside the written report, so they load straight into your tracker.

[03] Mobile application security

Android, iOS · 5–10 days

Static and dynamic analysis on real devices, plus the backend the app talks to. Most findings in a mobile engagement turn out to live server-side.

[+] Local storage Credentials, tokens and PII in preferences, SQLite, keychain and cache, including what survives a backup.
[+] Transport Certificate pinning strength, TLS configuration, and what the app does when pinning is stripped.
[+] Binary hardening Root and jailbreak detection, anti-tamper, debuggable builds and exported components.
[+] Secrets Hardcoded keys, API credentials and endpoints recovered from the shipped binary and its resources.
[+] IPC and deep links Exported activities, intent handling, universal links and custom URL schemes abused from another app.
[+] Backend The full API assessment above, run against the endpoints the app actually calls.
[x]

You receive: Device-level evidence, decompiled excerpts where relevant, and platform specific remediation for each finding.

[04] Network penetration testing

Internal and external · 5–15 days

Discovery, enumeration and exploitation across your perimeter and, where authorised, lateral movement from an assumed-breach foothold inside the network.

[+] External perimeter Everything reachable from the internet, including the hosts nobody remembers standing up.
[+] Service exploitation Known-vulnerable versions, misconfiguration and unauthenticated interfaces, confirmed by exploitation.
[+] Credential attacks Default and reused credentials, password spraying within agreed lockout limits, and credential reuse across tiers.
[+] Active Directory Kerberoasting, delegation abuse, ACL paths and privilege escalation routes to Domain Admin.
[+] Segmentation Whether your network zones hold, tested by attempting to cross them from each side.
[+] Egress What data can leave, and over which protocols, once an attacker is already inside.
[x]

You receive: Attack-path narrative alongside the finding list, so leadership can see how one weak host became domain compromise.

[05] Cloud security assessment

AWS, Azure, GCP · 4–10 days

Configuration reviewed against the provider benchmark, then tested rather than assumed. A policy that looks correct and a policy that holds are different things.

[+] Identity Over-permissive roles, wildcard policies, unused privileged principals and the escalation paths between them.
[+] Storage Public buckets, over-shared snapshots, unencrypted volumes and presigned URLs with no expiry.
[+] Network Security groups, peering, exposed management planes and services listening wider than intended.
[+] Secrets Keys in environment variables, build logs, images and repositories, plus rotation practice.
[+] Workloads Container and serverless configuration, image provenance and runtime privileges.
[+] Logging Whether the trail needed to investigate an incident actually exists and is retained.
[x]

You receive: Benchmark mapping (CIS or provider equivalent) with each deviation rated by real exploitability, not just compliance status.

[06] Compliance and audit support

Evidence, not checkboxes · runs alongside any engagement

Findings mapped to the control set your auditor asks about, with the artefacts they need attached to each one. This runs as an overlay on a technical engagement, not instead of it.

[+] OWASP Top 10 (2025) and ASVS levels 1–3, with per-requirement coverage stated explicitly.
[+] PCI-DSS Requirement 11.3 penetration testing, including segmentation validation and the retest evidence assessors expect.
[+] GDPR Article 32 technical measures, with findings framed in terms of risk to data subjects.
[+] HIPAA Security Rule technical safeguards for systems handling PHI.
[+] SOC 2 Evidence packs for the security criteria your auditor samples.
[+] Attestation A signed letter you can send to customers and prospects without handing over the full technical report.
[x]

You receive: A control-mapped evidence pack in addition to the standard report.

How engagements are shaped

Three formats cover almost everything. If none of them fit, say so on the scoping call and we will build one that does.

[+] Black box No credentials, no source, no documentation. Mirrors what an unauthenticated attacker sees. Fastest to scope, narrowest coverage.
[+] Grey box Credentials for each role plus architecture notes. The default, and the best value — typically surfaces 30–40% more than black box, concentrated in authorisation logic.
[+] White box Full source and infrastructure access. Deepest coverage, suited to pre-launch reviews and high-assurance systems.
[-] Continuous A retainer with quarterly full assessments and unlimited retests between them. Only worth it once you are shipping weekly.

What a scope sheet looks like

Scope is agreed in writing before day one and does not move without a signed change. Anything not on this sheet is not touched.
